MODULE 1

Know the aircraft

Learn the parts you will check before every flight, and what the aircraft can and cannot do.

570 gtakeoff weight
34 minmax flight time*
10 kmmax range (FCC)**
4K/60video, 48 MP photos

Key parts

  1. Forward vision system and front LEDs
  2. Propellers (low-noise, quick release)
  3. Gimbal and camera
  4. Landing gear with built-in antennas
  5. Backward vision system and status indicators
  6. Downward vision, infrared sensors, bottom light (underside)
  7. Intelligent Flight Battery, power button, microSD slot
Setup order: remove the gimbal protector, unfold the front arms before the rear arms, attach propellers, then power on. Powering on with the protector on or arms folded can affect the self-test.

* Tested at a steady 18 kph with no wind. ** Wide-open area, no interference.

MODULE 2

The remote controller

The default is Mode 2. Learn it before you take off.

Buttons that matter

  • Flight mode switch: Sport, Normal, Tripod
  • Pause/RTH button: press once to brake and hover; hold until it beeps to start Return to Home
  • Fn button: once toggles the bottom light; twice recenters the gimbal (default)
  • Gimbal dial, shutter, photo/video toggle

Start and stop the motors

  • Start: push both sticks to the bottom inner or outer corners, then release together
  • Stop after landing: hold the left stick down for 3 seconds
Never stop the motors in flight except in an emergency such as a collision or a stalled motor. The aircraft will fall.
MODULE 3

Battery and charging

A 3500 mAh smart battery. A full charge takes about 1 hour 35 minutes with the supplied charger.

Do

  • Fully charge before every flight
  • Let it cool before charging after a flight
  • In the cold, hover briefly to warm the battery and land at the first low-battery warning
  • Charge fully at least once every three months
  • Discharge to 30% or lower before transport

Remember

  • Press the power button once to check the level; press, then press and hold 2 seconds to power on or off
  • Auto-discharge to 96% after 1 idle day and to 60% after 5 days
  • Below 5% and idle for 6 hours it enters hibernation; charge to wake it
  • Do not remove the battery while the aircraft is powering on
MODULE 4

Flight modes and status lights

Know which mode you are in and what the rear lights are telling you.

Normal

GPS plus vision systems. Max 12 m/s. Obstacle sensing on. Best for shooting.

Sport

Max 19 m/s, very responsive. Obstacle sensing is off. Allow at least 30 m to brake.

Tripod

Based on Normal with slow speed (max 5 m/s) for smooth, precise shots.

ATTI mode: if GPS is weak and the vision systems are unavailable, the aircraft switches to Attitude mode automatically. It will drift with the wind, which is hazardous in tight spaces.

MODULE 5

Sensors and obstacle avoidance

Forward, backward, and downward vision systems plus infrared sensors help the aircraft hover and avoid obstacles.

Vision systems struggle over

  • Water, mirrors, and shiny or transparent surfaces
  • Plain single-color surfaces
  • Repeating patterns and moving surfaces
  • Very dark (under 10 lux) or very bright (over 40,000 lux) scenes
  • Thin objects such as branches, wires, and poles

APAS 3.0

In Normal mode the aircraft can steer around obstacles while you keep pushing forward or backward. It does not work sideways or in Intelligent Flight modes and some high-resolution video settings.

Keep sensors clean: check for stickers, dust, and water before each takeoff and wipe with a soft cloth. Do not use alcohol-based cleaners.
MODULE 6

Return to Home

The aircraft flies back to the home point recorded at takeoff. Set the RTH altitude before every flight.

Smart RTH

You trigger it: hold the RTH button until it beeps, or tap the icon in DJI Fly. Under 5 m from home it lands at once; 5 to 50 m it flies home at the current altitude.

Low battery RTH

A 10-second countdown, then it returns on its own. Cancelling risks a crash. If the battery can only cover the descent, it lands and cannot be cancelled.

Failsafe RTH

If the controller signal is lost for more than 11 seconds, it retraces its route backward for 50 m, then flies home.

Limits: during RTH the aircraft cannot see obstacles at its sides or above. A weak GPS signal, strong wind, or a GEO zone can stop it reaching home.
MODULE 7

DJI Fly app and camera

Use DJI Fly for every flight. Activation needs an internet connection the first time.

Camera view, top bar

  • Flight mode and system status
  • Battery level and remaining flight time
  • Controller signal, vision status, GPS strength
  • Settings: Safety, Control, Camera, Transmission, About

Bottom bar

  • D = distance from home, H = height
  • Horizontal and vertical speed
  • Map, aircraft orientation, microSD time left

Smart shooting

  • FocusTrack: Spotlight 2.0, ActiveTrack 3.0, Point of Interest 3.0
  • QuickShots: Dronie, Rocket, Circle, Helix, Boomerang, Asteroid
  • Hyperlapse: Free, Circle, Course Lock, Waypoints
  • Pano: Sphere, 180°, Wide, Vertical

Boomerang needs a 30 m radius and 10 m clear above. Asteroid needs 40 m behind and 50 m above. Use a UHS-I Speed Grade 3 microSD card; single clips are limited to 30 minutes.

In Settings, Safety: set max altitude, max distance, and the RTH altitude. Keep Obstacle Detection on unless you have a reason to turn it off. Avoid FocusTrack near people, animals, wires, and glass.
MODULE 7B

App tour: reading the flight screen

Six real screens from a training flight. This control app is laid out differently from the DJI Fly screens in the manual, but the information is the same. Use these to practice finding each item.

Practice: before each flight, find the battery level, GPS count, flight mode, obstacle distances, and any warning banner. If a banner or a red status appears, read it fully and fix it before takeoff.
MODULE 8

Pre-flight and first flight

Tick each item as you go. Your progress shows below.

Pre-flight checklist

First flight steps

  1. Place the aircraft on open, flat ground with the status light facing you
  2. Power on the aircraft and controller; open DJI Fly
  3. Wait for the green blink: home point recorded
  4. Take off gently or use auto takeoff (hovers at 1.2 m)
  5. Hover and practice slow, gentle stick inputs
  6. Land, hold the left stick down to stop the motors
  7. Power off the aircraft, then the controller

Where not to fly

  • Wind over 10 m/s, rain, snow, or fog
  • Near high-voltage lines, substations, base stations
  • Within 5 m of structures; keep 3 m above water
  • Near airports, highways, rail, city centers, or GEO zones
  • Beyond your line of sight
Compass calibration is needed when you fly more than 50 km from your last location, after 30 days without flying, or when the app or lights warn you. Never calibrate near metal, cars, or reinforced concrete. Follow all FAA and local rules; you are responsible for flying legally.
